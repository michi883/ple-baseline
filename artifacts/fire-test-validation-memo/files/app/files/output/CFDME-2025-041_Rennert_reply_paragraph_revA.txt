Bianca — the straight answer for the launch materials is no: the simulation cannot be described as “validated against the certified test.” Red Butte's certified result (report RBFMT-25-0918) is 118 min, governed by the single-point criterion. Our companion simulation predicts 131 min, which overstates the endurance by 13 min. Our acceptance limit is 10 % of the certified value, or 11.8 min, and the combined validation uncertainty is ±9 min. The difference exceeds both, so we are treating it as a model finding and reworking the model. We can't use the 129 min figure, because a post-test correction worked up with your thermocouple vendor is not a laboratory revision. Only a reissued Red Butte report, backed by a calibration certificate and stating that it supersedes the original, could replace the 118 min, and only Ironwood can change the rating. Until then the certified rating is 1 hour, and nothing on file supports a 2-hour figure. Finally, ASTM E119 is a standard furnace test, not a wildfire exposure, so no E119 result can be cited as wildfire performance.
