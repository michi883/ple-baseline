I checked Rev.1 as supplemented by Rev.2 (KE-L-2026-0713), as Kesterline asked. The groundwater and ground inputs it uses agree with the logged record: water table El. 742.0, confined head El. 738.0 (logged maximum 738.3), aquifer top El. 720.0, leaving a 6.0 ft clay plug below subgrade. Its load and final-subgrade base-heave arithmetic (R2.2, R2.3) also checks.

**Why it isn't verified:**
- **Toe embedment fails.** It uses an undrained strength of 1,400 psf; the geotechnical report gives 900–1,100 psf. On that range the toe factor of safety is 0.31–0.81, below 1.0, and there is no long-term (drained) check.
- **Anchors, bending and deflection fall with it.** The anchors are bonded entirely in clay (about El. 737.0–728.7) using a bond stress taken for sand and gravel. The predicted 1.3 in deflection exceeds Rev.1's own 1.20 in stop-excavation level.
- **Base stability depends entirely on undesigned pumping.** Without the relief wells the factor of safety at subgrade is 0.58. There is no sealed dewatering design, no aquifer test and no discharge permit, and the Block A dewatering plan expressly forbids this reliance. The 72-hour hold at El. 732.0 and the stop trigger at El. 727.0 both let the factor fall below the required 1.50 (the hold should be at about El. 735.6).
- **Sheets would pierce the clay layer unsealed.** Driving to El. 719.5 penetrates the clay layer separating the shallow water from the aquifer, with no seal and no cutoff credit taken.
- **Rev.2 contradicts the Rev.1 sections it keeps.** Its table of which Rev.1 sections are superseded uses different section numbers and titles from Rev.1 itself. Sections left "in force" still carry the dry-excavation assumption, the El. 722.0 tip and conflicting lock-off loads.
- **The design ignores what is already built.** It assumes open ground at El. 750.0. Drawing S-101 Rev.4 puts the pit inside the cast basement mat, with the grid-H row of Block B piles on its north edge. The Contractor's daily reports show a finished slab and services in that area.

**Checks that can be carried forward:** stratigraphy, water levels, unit weights, the loads above subgrade, the base-heave check at final subgrade only, and the steel capacities (sheet section, strand, wale). Nothing else carries forward, including the toe, anchors, bending demand, deflection, triggers, the pumping scheme and the Rev.1 sliding/overturning check.

**Effect on the Block B permanent work:** pumping would lower the aquifer head by 11.5–11.8 ft beneath the 48 piles and the mat. The memo estimates the resulting clay settlement at roughly 0.25–0.7 in over the pumping period, about 1 in at 12 months and 2.2–2.4 in if sustained. That adds drag load to piles whose downdrag is already unresolved (Item 049) and differential settlement against the 1.0 in / 0.5 in tolerances. The sheets, anchors and relief wells would also clash with the grid-H piles and caps, the mat and the line-5 wall, and a pumping failure could heave the plug beside the pile group.
